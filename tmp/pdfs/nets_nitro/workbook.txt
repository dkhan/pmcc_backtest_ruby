                          The Buy to Sell Ratio (BSR)
• Definition: Ratio of Buy to Sell ratings of all the stocks in the database.
• Purpose: Indicates the strength of any major market rally or retreat.
   When BSR > 1.00 & Rising, it indicates the market rally is healthy and broadening.
   When BSR < 1.00 & Falling, it indicates the market is not healthy and is continuing to weaken.
   When BSR < 0.20, the market is searching for a bottom.

                       Confirmed Calls (U.S. Market)

  1.00.
• A downturn is confirmed when the Price of the VVC moves
  down for two consecutive 5-day periods and the BSR is less
  than 1.00.

        • RT measures momentum. It acts as a radar for stock price movement.

                            The 50-50 Strategy

    The 50-50 Strategy is a disciplined trend-following
strategy designed to help traders enter established trends
                after a healthy pullback.
Best suited for:
• Swing traders (days to weeks)
• Position traders (weeks to months)
• Longer-term investors seeking lower-risk entries




                               Core Principles

                 The 50-50 Strategy by Dr. Charles Schaap

The 50-50 Strategy combines trend, momentum, and timing to identify
high-probability trade setups.
The strategy focuses on four key elements:
•   Trade in the direction of the primary trend.
•   Wait for price to pull back to the 50-day Moving Average.
•   Use the 20-period RSI to confirm momentum.
•   Apply the same process to daily, weekly, or intraday charts.
               Relative Strength Index (RSI)

Relative Strength Index (RSI)
• The Relative Strength Index (RSI), developed by J. Welles
  Wilder Jr., is one of the most widely used momentum
  indicators in technical analysis.
• RSI measures the speed and strength of price movement on a
  scale from 0 to 100, helping traders identify periods when
  momentum may be overextended.




               Relative Strength Index (RSI)

Reading RSI
RSI helps identify when buying or selling pressure may be reaching an
extreme.
General Guidelines
• Above 70 – Momentum may be becoming overextended (overbought).
• Below 30 – Selling pressure may be nearing exhaustion (oversold).
These levels should be used as context—not automatic buy or sell signals.
                         Adjusting RSI Sensitivity

The RSI lookback period determines how quickly the indicator responds to
changes in price.
Longer Lookback Period
•   Responds more slowly to price movement.
•   Produces fewer trading signals.
•   Reduces short-term fluctuations.
•   Provides more stable readings.
Shorter Lookback Period
•   Responds more quickly to price movement.
•   Produces more trading signals.
•   Reacts more strongly to short-term fluctuations.
•   May generate more false signals.
The appropriate setting depends on the trader’s timeframe and desired level of sensitivity.




           RSI Can Also Measure Trend Strength

RSI is commonly used to identify overbought and oversold conditions, but
it can also help confirm the direction of a trend.
Using the 20-period RSI:
• Above 50 indicates bullish momentum.
• Below 50 indicates bearish momentum.
• A move from below 50 to above 50 signals improving momentum.
• A move from above 50 to below 50 signals weakening momentum.
Within the 50/50 Strategy, the 50 level helps determine whether short-
term momentum supports the broader trend.
   Defining the Intermediate and Long-Term Trends

      The 50-day and 200-day Moving Averages provide a quick visual
                  assessment of a stock’s overall trend.
50-Day Moving Average
• Represents the intermediate-term trend and helps identify potential pullback
  opportunities.
200-Day Moving Average
• Represents the longer-term trend and provides broader market context.
• When both Moving Averages are rising, the stock is generally in an established
  uptrend. When both are falling, the stock is generally in an established downtrend.
• The relationship between these two Moving Averages creates the foundation of the
  50/50 Strategy.




                      50-50 Strategy (Bullish)

    A bullish setup begins with an established uptrend and a controlled
                                 pullback.
Entry Checklist
 50-day and 200-day Moving Averages are rising.
 Price pulls back toward the 50-day Moving Average.
 20-period RSI remains above 50.
 Enter using a buy stop above the previous day's high.
Initial Stop
Place the initial stop $0.25 below the reversal candle low.

Consider taking profits or exiting the position when one or more
of the following occurs:
• RSI reaches or exceeds 70.
• Price closes below the 50-day Moving Average.
• A bearish reversal candlestick forms.
• The trend shows signs of breaking down.

                  50-50 Strategy (Bearish)

Trading Established Downtrends
The Bearish 50-50 Strategy applies the same principles as the bullish setup
but in reverse. Rather than buying pullbacks in an uptrend, the objective is
to sell short after a rally into resistance within an established downtrend.
Ideal for:
• Swing traders
• Active traders
• Bearish market environments




                          Core Principles

The Bearish 50-50 Strategy combines trend, momentum, and
timing to identify high-probability short-selling opportunities.

The strategy focuses on four key elements:
• Trade in the direction of the primary downtrend.
• Wait for price to rally into the 50-day Moving Average.
• Use the 20-period RSI to confirm bearish momentum.
• Enter only after price shows signs of reversing lower.
                   Defining the Downtrend

Before considering any short trade, confirm that the primary trend is
bearish.
Trend Checklist
 200-day Moving Average is declining.
 50-day Moving Average is declining.
 Price is trading below both Moving Averages.
 The 50-day MA remains below the 200-day MA.

    The greater the separation between the Moving Averages, the stronger the
                             downtrend tends to be.




            Confirming Momentum with RSI


Momentum should support the bearish trend.
Look for:
• 20-period RSI below 50
• RSI remaining below 50 during the rally
• Momentum beginning to weaken as price approaches the 50-day
  Moving Average
     A rally with weak RSI often signals that sellers remain in control.
                          Bearish Entry Rules

A short setup begins with an established downtrend followed by a
controlled rally.
Entry Checklist
 50-day and 200-day Moving Averages are declining.
 Price rallies toward the 50-day Moving Average.
 20-period RSI remains below 50.
 Price forms a bearish reversal.
 Place a sell stop below the previous day's low.
Initial Stop
Place the initial stop $0.25 above the high of the reversal candle.




                         Managing the Trade

Consider covering the short position when one or more of the
following occurs:
• RSI falls to 30 or below.
• Price closes above the 50-day Moving Average.
• A bullish reversal candlestick develops.
• The downtrend begins to weaken.
  Remain disciplined and avoid allowing profitable trades to reverse into
                                   losses.
